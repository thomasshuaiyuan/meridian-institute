# 此心安处 · A Compass for Myself

*Oct 27, 2026 · @thomas*

## Why this exists

I wrote this at 25 so that the person I become never trades away who I am now. I live between China and the West, and I can see both sides of the story. That is my strength in good times and my exposure in bad ones.

When I feel lost, pressured to choose a side, or tempted to stay quiet for my career, I come back here.

## What history taught me

Power that rests on a symbol nobody may question is fragile, and people caught between two powers are judged by what they do when forced to choose.

- **Mao as 金字招牌.** Those at the top hold up the signboard to protect themselves; those below hold it up as a shield. What matters is who is allowed to read what is written on it. A system is a real republic only when it no longer needs a signboard nobody may touch.
- **Institutions decide what happens after a fall.** Eastern Europe had unions, churches and courts behind the signboard, so change held. The Middle East had nothing behind it, so the army or a new strongman took over.
- **Xinhai had two paths.** The revolutionaries changed who held power, and many died young. The builders — 蔡元培, 张伯苓, 梅贻琦, 詹天佑 — built universities and railways that outlasted every regime.
- **Wang Jingwei** began as a hero ("引刀成一快") and ended serving a regime he knew was wrong. Conviction at 27 is no guarantee at 57.
- **黄祖廷** knew right from wrong and chose loyalty to the emperor anyway. His tragedy was not belonging to two worlds. It was handing his conscience to someone else, and trying to atone only when it was too late.

## My anchor: 致良知

My conscience is the final authority — not China, not the West, not an employer, not a flag. When I can't answer "which side am I on?", I ask questions that do have answers:

1. Am I telling the truth?
2. Am I helping people's health and lives?
3. Is my science honest?
4. Am I treating people fairly?

I answer to my patients, my students and the truth in my data. Being between two worlds is not a gap; it is a place of its own, the way it was for 容闎 and 詹天佑.

此心安处是吾乡 — home is where the heart is at peace: my work, the people I love, the values I keep.

## Lines I will not cross, for anyone

These are decided now, with a clear head, so I never have to decide them under pressure.

- I will not do work designed to harm people. Pathogen science is dual-use; I remember Unit 731's doctors were trained physicians who obeyed.
- I will not falsify, suppress or bend data, whoever asks and whatever it costs my career.
- I will not stay silent about an outbreak or a risk to people's health that I know is real.
- I will not betray the people who trust me — family, students, collaborators — to prove loyalty to any side.
- I will not let ambition become the goal. Strength, money and status are tools for building, never the point.

## Tripwires: when I leave

I leave early, on a commercial flight, the moment any of these happens. Leaving early and being wrong costs a plane ticket; leaving late may not be possible.

- My government tells its citizens to leave, or orders embassy families out.
- Major airlines suspend routes.
- Clear signs of mobilization or sudden capital controls.
- A scientist in a non-political field is detained for national security reasons.
- I am asked to do something that crosses one of my lines.

**What keeps the exit open:**
- One passport, nationality status unambiguous
- Registered with my government's service for citizens abroad
- Real savings, an address and a network outside China
- Key documents and research backed up where I can reach them from anywhere
- A family plan for how we reach each other and where we meet in a crisis

## How I build: the 蔡元培 path

I serve China by building institutions that outlast me, not by defiance. A belief worth living for, not dying for.

- **Science first.** Honest data about outbreaks protects real people, and a culture of truth-telling is a form of the Xinhai ideal the system can sometimes accept.
- **Build what lasts.** Research institutions, trained students, open methods and code — the "institutions behind the signboard."
- **Private convictions, public role.** I can believe in a republic, rule of law and people supervising their government, and live those values inside my institution: open inquiry, merit over connections, independent thinking.
- **Stay portable.** International publications, collaborators abroad, a Western postdoc or foothold. Builders survive by being able to wait out bad periods, as 梅贻琦 moved Tsinghua to Kunming and kept teaching.
- **Be careful with my name.** Think before attaching it to political writing or sending it on channels that are read by others.

## Questions to reread every year

On my birthday, I answer these honestly in writing and compare with last year.

1. Is what I am building still what I set out to build, or has protecting my position become the goal?
2. Have I stayed silent about something I should have said? Have I bent anything I should not have?
3. Is anyone asking me to prove loyalty in a way that conflicts with my conscience?
4. Are my exits still open, and are my tripwires still the right ones?
5. Would the 25-year-old who wrote this be proud of me, and would 黄祖廷 recognize his mistake in me?
6. Am I at peace? If not, who have I talked to about it?
