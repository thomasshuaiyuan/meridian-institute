D2 · simulated

The last two weeks are always wrong.

A sequence is deposited some time after the case occurs. So at any real-time snapshot the recent end of the genomic record is not sparse by accident — it is systematically incomplete, and the deficit grows the closer you get to today.

A birth–death skyline fit with a constant sampling proportion cannot tell that apart from a genuine fall in transmission. It reads the missing sequences as a real decline and pushes R(t) down exactly where the answer matters. D2 replaces the constant with a function of time since the event — the probability a sequence from day t has arrived by today.

The correction is not a smoother and it is not a forecast. It is a statement about what the sampling proportion actually is: not a scalar fitted once, but p × G(today − t), where G is the deposition-delay distribution. Where G is still near zero the honest output is no estimate at all — the grey zone on the right — rather than a confident one.
