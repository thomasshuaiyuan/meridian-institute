Delay-NPE · schematic

Where the training set ends.

Amortised inference is usually described in two steps — train once, then answer instantly. The description leaves out the step that decides whether the answer is worth anything, which is how well the training set covered the question being asked. These are the three steps as geometry: a genealogy, the surface learned from many of them, and the density of support that surface rests on.

The shapes are the argument. None of the three carries a number, because the point each one makes is structural and survives without one.

IThe data object is a shape

Inference does not read sequences. It reads the tree they imply — the order in which lineages split and how long they waited — and everything downstream is a statement about that shape. Drawn with time as depth and turned slowly, the genealogy is visibly a three-dimensional object; the flat rectangular tree in every paper is one projection of it, chosen for printing.

The tips light as they are reached. Each is a sequence with a collection date, and the spacing between them in time is most of the signal.

IIMany trees become one surface

Fitting a sampler to each dataset does not scale across a surveillance network. The alternative is to simulate a great many genealogies spanning the range of parameters you expect, and train once on that collection, so the estimator learns a map from tree shape to posterior.

What the training produces is a surface over the parameter space. The tips leaving the tree and settling onto it is that transfer: individual histories stop being separate fits and become positions on one learned object. Afterwards each new dataset is a single forward pass across it. The cost side of this trade is laid out in Pay once, then infer for free.

IIISupport is a quantity, and the edge is a choice

A query that lands where training was dense gets an answer the training supports. One that lands out in the thin fog gets an answer too — just as fast, and just as narrow. Nothing in a forward pass hesitates, because the operation it performs is identical either way.

What the volume shows is that there is no boundary sitting there to be found. There is a density: thick where the simulated training set put many genealogies, thinning outward, never quite reaching zero. The grain at the rim is the point — out there the field is sampled too sparsely to support anything, and it degrades rather than stopping.

So a detector has to compute that density separately and compare it against a cutoff somebody picked. Drag the cutoff and the fog does not change; only the verdict does. Probes flip as the line crosses them. Push it high enough and even well-covered queries get routed to the slow path, which is the cost of being careful; leave it low and queries the estimator cannot answer sail through, which is the cost of not being.

The density field here is invented. It is a saddle with a smooth falloff and some noise, chosen because the thinning edge reads clearly when it turns — not a picture of any trained estimator's actual support. The claim the page makes is about the structure: that support is continuous, that queries outside it are answered anyway, and that the in-or-out verdict is a threshold someone chose. Nothing here shows where a real cutoff should sit.

What would make this a figure rather than a schematic: a trained Delay-NPE estimator and its real support density, with the probes drawn from the surveillance record instead of placed by hand. Then the fog would be the estimator's own, and where it thins would be a finding. Until that runs, this documents a mechanism, which is the smaller claim and the only one it supports.
