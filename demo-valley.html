Fitness-valley escape · simulated

Why lesions multiply instead of adding.

An attenuated construct sits in a fitness well. Reversion means crossing a valley: intermediate genotypes are less fit than either end, so most attempts fall back. Escape is not a typical event, it is a rare one — and rare events have a rate that depends on barrier height exponentially rather than linearly.

Below, several hundred lineages diffuse under selection and drift. Deepen the valley by one lesion and watch the crossings thin out. The second panel does not assert the law — it measures it from the simulation and plots it on a log axis, where an exponential becomes a straight line.

What the straight line is, and is not. It is a property of the dynamics: in a log plot, exponential dependence on barrier height appears as a line, and its slope is set by how noisy the process is relative to the barrier. That is mathematics and it holds here by construction. What it is not is evidence about any real construct. Turning this into a usable quantity means estimating a real fitness landscape from passage data and showing that measured reversion frequencies follow the same line — and that is the part that has not been done.

The reason the picture matters for vaccine design: if reversion rate falls exponentially in barrier height, then architecture dominates count. Two lesions that each contribute a deep, independent barrier are worth far more than four shallow ones sharing a route back, and the regulatory habit of counting attenuating sites rather than measuring the barrier between them is exactly what the exponent would replace.
