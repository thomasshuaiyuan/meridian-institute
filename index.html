The Problem

A documented gap in vaccine safety science.

Live-attenuated vaccines are among the most effective prophylactic tools in infectious disease prevention. They are also the least characterized in one critical respect: no standardized, reproducible method exists to quantify how far a candidate sits from reversion to pathogenicity.

The consequences are documented. Circulating vaccine-derived polioviruses tripled in incidence over a five-year period. HSV attenuation programs have advanced without a quantitative framework for stability assessment. Regulatory dossiers on genetic stability remain descriptive rather than computational.

Every live-attenuated vaccine developer faces this gap at some point in their development program. No independent scientific resource currently closes it.

Our Approach

Barrier metrics: a reproducible genome-level standard.

We compute genome-scale conservation scores, mutational constraint distributions, and reversion probability estimates from curated sequence corpora. The result is a pathogen-specific dossier that answers the question developers and regulators need answered: which residues are under selective constraint, which mutational pathways to virulence remain accessible, and how much evolutionary distance separates the candidate from its nearest pathogenic neighbor in sequence space.

Conservation Analysis
Position-level entropy and covariation across curated genome corpora. Identifies sites under functional constraint across thousands of clinical isolates.
Reversion Probability
Bayesian estimates of per-site reversion likelihood under replication pressure, calibrated against documented reversion events in the literature.
Sequence Distance
Genomic distance from candidate to the nearest characterized pathogenic sequence — a quantitative margin of attenuation for the full genome.

Founder

Shuai (Thomas) Yuan

Founder & Director

Thomas is a doctoral researcher in computational infectious disease epidemiology at the University of Hong Kong, where he works in the Dhanasekaran Laboratory at the HKU-Pasteur Research Pole. His research develops computational methods for phylodynamic inference and genomic surveillance of respiratory and neurotropic viruses.

His founding work on barrier metrics grew from a systematic analysis of HSV-1 genome conservation across more than 400 clinical isolates — the first genome-scale quantitative characterization of reversion constraint in a live-attenuated herpesvirus candidate. He holds an MS in Epidemiology from Columbia University Mailman School of Public Health and a BA in Biology from Washington University in St. Louis.
