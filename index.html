The Problem

Biological risk is distributed unequally.
Measured unequally.
The two compound.

AI is already collapsing what’s compressible: design-space search, inference under bias, statistical-strength transfer across settings. These problems shrink toward compute. They will keep shrinking.

What does not compress: evolutionary time. Data-generation time. Institutional time. The largest outbreaks unfold in the least-resourced settings — no model can retrospectively surveil the unsampled. Live-attenuated vaccines play out their reversion risk in real bodies over real transmission chains — no simulation prices that risk before release. Antigenic drift erodes vaccine-induced immunity on timescales no trial can follow. Antimicrobial resistance climbs evolutionary trajectories that are measurable before clinical emergence. Zoonotic spillover crosses host range through phylodynamic processes that give weeks of early signal — if genomic surveillance can see it.

Meridian works at that boundary. Pre-registered evolutionary-risk frameworks, observation-bias quantification, and immune-landscape reconstruction — the layer between the model output and the claim that must survive regulatory or peer scrutiny.

Founder

Shuai (Thomas) Yuan

Founder & Director

Thomas is a doctoral researcher in computational infectious disease epidemiology at the University of Hong Kong, where he works in the Dhanasekaran Laboratory at the HKU-Pasteur Research Pole. His research develops computational methods for phylodynamic inference and genomic surveillance of respiratory and neurotropic viruses.

His founding work on barrier metrics grew from a systematic analysis of HSV-2 genome conservation across 574 clinical isolates — a genome-scale quantitative characterization of reversion constraint in live-attenuated herpesvirus vaccine candidates. The construct ranking spans both HSV-2 candidates (HB01, RVx201, HSV-529, 0ΔRING) and the HSV-1(F)-backbone candidate VC2, evaluated against a separate HSV-1 alignment panel.

He is co-founder of Drugdu, a cross-border pharmaceutical procurement platform operating across 50+ countries, and serves as Computational Biology Lead at Hyper Biotech, where he designed the programme’s first pre-registered animal safety study with go/no-go criteria fixed before data collection began. He holds an MS in Epidemiology from Columbia University Mailman School of Public Health and a BA in Biology from Washington University in St. Louis.

Work With Us

One semester. One artifact.

What this is. An independent research collaboration, currently unfunded — not a degree program, not employment. The pitch is the problem and the standard. The people working here now become its foundation if it ever becomes those things.

The problem. Health intelligence scales with wealth: the largest outbreaks in the least-resourced settings are the least observed, in a way we can measure. Live vaccines carry evolutionary risk priced nowhere before deployment. Evolution is predicted almost nowhere and observed through biased measurement everywhere. Inference problems are solvable.

The bet. AI is collapsing the cost of analysis toward zero. What remains scarce is credibility: measurement that exists before inference, risk priced before release, judgment that survives scrutiny. When analysis is cheap, pre-registration is how credibility compounds.

The standard. Every analysis pre-registered. Every claim traced to evidence. Every correction numbered and public. Slower per week, faster per year — nothing has to be defended twice. If you want early work that referees cannot kill, this is the environment.

Open problems — each sized to one semester, each attached to a publication:

  • Optimal allocation of scarce sequencing when surveillance effort scales sublinearly with incidence — where does the next genome buy the most information?
  • Benchmark protein language model fitness scores against genome-scale constraint maps from 574 clinical isolates — does predicted evolutionary fitness match empirically derived reversion barriers?
  • Bias correction for seroprevalence inference when sampling frames differ across populations.

What you get. Co-authorship on artifacts, not acknowledgments. Training in a verification workflow most labs don’t teach. Work you can put in front of any committee without flinching.

The ask. One problem from the list, or one you bring that survives our scrutiny. One artifact with your name on it.
