Methods

Five demonstrations, each saying what it knows.

A method is easier to argue with when you can move it. These are interactive demonstrations of the machinery behind the research directions — not results. None of them runs on real data, and each page states in its own header what it does run on — simulated in four cases, drawn in one — because a figure separated from its caption is how a simulation becomes a claim.

The tag on each card says how far the underlying method has actually got.

The last two weeks are always wrong

Sequences arrive after the cases they came from, so the recent end of the genomic record is systematically incomplete. A birth–death skyline with a constant sampling proportion reads that as a fall in transmission and pushes R(t) down exactly where it matters. Move the delay slider and the bias moves with it.

Pay once, then infer for free

One sampler run per surveillance unit per week does not scale to a hundred and fifty national centres. Amortised inference moves the cost up front: simulate once, train once, and every dataset afterwards is a forward pass. The second panel shows the price — an estimator asked about data outside its training support answers just as fast and just as confidently, and can be wrong.

Why lesions multiply instead of adding

Reversion means crossing a fitness valley, and crossings are rare events whose rate depends on barrier height exponentially rather than linearly. A thousand lineages diffuse in the well; the second panel measures the escape rate from the simulation and plots it on a log axis, where an exponential is a straight line.

How much diversity survives, and where it is lost

Reducing a genome corpus to cluster representatives invites one question: why that threshold? A decay curve answers how much tree length survives and cannot answer where. The ring around the tree records what each sector kept, and the date strip records whether the time span survived too.

Where the training set ends

A genealogy is a shape; training on many of them produces a surface; and the surface has an edge. A query past that edge is answered just as fast and just as narrowly as any other, because a forward pass cannot hesitate. Three movements, drawn as geometry and carrying no numbers, because each point they make is structural.

What is missing from all five: real data. Each becomes an argument about the world rather than about a method on the day it runs on the corpus or the surveillance record it was built for, and each page says which inputs that would take. Until then they demonstrate mechanisms, which is a smaller claim and the only one they support.
