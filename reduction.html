Corpus reduction · simulated

How much diversity survives, and where it is lost.

Reducing a genome corpus to cluster representatives invites one question: why that threshold? A decay curve answers how much tree length survives. It cannot answer where. A reduction that keeps 90% of total diversity while emptying one clade is a bad reduction, and the curve scores it as a good one.

So the tips keep their original angular slot — a gap is a real loss — and the ring records, sector by sector, how much of each region is still standing.

Relative tree length

The rule fits in one sentence: the minimum pairwise distance in an additive tree is always a sister pair, so at each step take the closest cherry and drop whichever of its tips sits on the shorter terminal branch. Random removal sheds diversity roughly in proportion to tips lost; targeted pruning does not. The gap between the two lines is why a reduced corpus can carry most of the original signal. The ring is how you show it did so evenly — which is the claim a reviewer will actually test.
